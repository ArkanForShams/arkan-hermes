# Executive Reporting Skill

Executive-level reporting and steering-committee procedures: steering pack design, exception-based narratives, decision framing, and the pre-read discipline. The narrative layer above pmo-reporting metrics - this skill turns data into decisions.

## When to Use
- Preparing steering committees, executive updates, board inputs
- Writing status narratives executives actually read
- Framing decisions, asks, and escalations for senior audiences
- Don't use for: metric definitions and dashboards (pmo-reporting), meeting prep drills (executive-coach)

## Procedure
1. **Steering pack skeleton.** One page: where we are (3 numbers vs plan) -> what changed -> risks/decisions needed -> next milestone. Detail to appendix. Completion criterion: pack <= 2 pages + appendix.
2. **Exception-based narrative.** Green = one line. Spend the words on red/amber: cause, impact, options, recommendation. Completion criterion: every red item carries options, not just bad news.
3. **Decision framing.** Each ask: decision needed, by when, options with trade-offs, recommendation. No decision buried in prose. Completion criterion: asks numbered with deadlines.
4. **Pre-read discipline.** Send 24h ahead; meeting starts with "questions on the pack", not a re-read. Completion criterion: pack sent on time; meeting opens with decisions, not status.
5. **Numbers discipline.** Three numbers maximum per topic; every number sourced from the metric dictionary (pmo-reporting). No unsourced figures in front of executives. Completion criterion: figures traceable.
6. **Follow-up loop.** Decisions logged with owners and dates in the decision log; next pack opens with last decisions' status. Completion criterion: decision register current.
7. **Tone calibration.** For Shams: formal, tactful, no escalation language; problems framed with paths forward; credit distributed to teams. Completion criterion: draft re-read for tone before sending.

## Quick Reference
- **Executives read for: what changed, what you need, what it costs.** Structure follows that order.
- **Never bring a problem without options;** never bring options without a recommendation.
- **The decision log is the steering committee's memory;** without it, decisions get re-litigated.
- **Bad news travels first and in person** where possible; the pack confirms, not surprises.

## Pitfalls
- **Data dumps:** 30 slides of status. Executives disengage and decide nothing.
- **Surprise reds:** first appearance of a red at steering destroys trust. Ramp expectations early.
- **Hiding behind hedging:** "challenges remain" without options wastes the room's time.
- **Skipping the ask:** reporting without a decision request is a newsletter.

## Verification
- Packs delivered 24h ahead consistently; decisions logged with owners/dates.
- Every red/amber has options + recommendation; every number traceable to the dictionary.
- Steering meetings open with decisions, not re-reads.

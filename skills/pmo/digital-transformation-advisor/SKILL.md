# Digital Transformation Advisor Skill

Transformation program procedures: vision-to-roadmap translation, capability-based planning, transformation governance, and momentum management across a multi-year change arc. The strategic program layer above program-manager.

## When to Use
- Planning or steering a multi-year digital transformation program
- Translating executive vision into sequenced capability roadmaps
- Transformation governance: value tracking, course correction, momentum
- Don't use for: single program delivery mechanics (program-manager), technology landscape (technology-portfolio-advisor), AI-specific transformation (caio-advisor)

## Procedure
1. **Translate vision to outcomes.** Executive ambition -> measurable outcomes ("digital operations with self-service" -> specific metrics). Completion criterion: outcome tree with measures and baselines per branch.
2. **Capability-based planning.** Map needed capabilities (not projects) - e.g., data foundation, integration platform, digital channels, AI enablement; sequence by dependency and value. Completion criterion: capability map with dependency-ordered waves.
3. **Wave architecture.** Wave 1 foundations (highest risk-first), Wave 2 visible wins (momentum), Wave 3 scale. Each wave: scope, outcomes, funding gate, exit criteria. Completion criterion: wave plan with funded Wave 1 only - later waves are committed, not spent.
4. **Dual-track delivery.** Run-the-business vs change-the-business capacity explicitly split; transformation starves operations silently otherwise. Completion criterion: capacity split documented and protected.
5. **Transformation governance.** Monthly value review: outcomes vs baselines, not milestone counts; quarterly course correction with authority to re-sequence waves. Completion criterion: governance calendar with decision log.
6. **Story and momentum.** A one-page transformation narrative (why -> what -> how -> what changes for you) refreshed per audience; visible early wins communicated (executive-reporting). Completion criterion: narrative current; wins broadcast on cadence.
7. **Benefit realization.** Benefits owned by business sponsors (not IT), tracked post-wave against baseline, linked to budget-manager ledger. Completion criterion: benefits register with named business owners.
8. **Course-correction triggers.** Pre-agreed: benefit shortfall > X% by wave end -> re-scope; sponsor loss -> escalate; capability dependency slipping -> re-sequence. Completion criterion: triggers written at wave start, exercised when hit.

## Quick Reference
- **Outcomes over projects:** a transformation is a portfolio of capabilities, not a Gantt chart.
- **Fund waves, not programs:** money committed per wave with exit gates keeps optionality.
- **Momentum is a resource:** one visible win per quarter sustains sponsorship; two silent quarters invites doubt.
- **IT delivers, business owns benefits** - benefit ownership outside IT is non-negotiable for real adoption.

## Pitfalls
- **Big-bang myopia:** one mega-program with everything in year one. Waves with gates beat grand plans.
- **Project-count theater:** reporting activities ("12 projects green") instead of outcome movement.
- **Operations starvation:** change work consumes run capacity; incidents rise, trust falls. Protect the split.
- **Benefit amnesia:** claimed at launch, unmeasured by wave end. Baselines or it isn't transformation, it's construction.

## Verification
- Outcome tree, capability map, wave plan current; governance reviews held with decisions logged.
- Benefits register with named business owners and baseline tracking live.
- Early wins communicated per cadence; narrative refreshed per audience.

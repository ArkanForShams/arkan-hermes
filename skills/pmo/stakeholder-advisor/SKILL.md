# Stakeholder Advisor Skill

Stakeholder strategy procedures: mapping, engagement planning, steering committee management, and relationship maintenance across executives, sponsors, and business partners - built for Shams's diplomatic, peacemaker style.

## When to Use
- Mapping stakeholders for a project, program, or transformation
- Planning engagement for difficult sponsors or conflicting interests
- Running steering committees and business-relationship rhythms
- Don't use for: executive pack writing (executive-reporting), personal presence drills (executive-coach)

## Procedure
1. **Map the field.** Stakeholder x interest x influence x current stance (champion/supporter/neutral/skeptic/blocker); include hidden influencers (the EA, the long-tenured lead). Completion criterion: map complete with stance evidence, refreshed monthly.
2. **Engagement plan per stakeholder.** What they need (outcomes, optics, risk control), what they fear, cadence, channel, message frame. Completion criterion: plan per high-influence stakeholder; no generic comms.
3. **Sponsor activation.** Confirm each sponsor's role: visible backing moments, decision authority, unblocking duty. An absent sponsor is a schedule risk - surface it early. Completion criterion: sponsor charter agreed (what/when/decisions).
4. **Conflicting interests.** Where stakeholders want opposite things: surface the trade-off to the deciding forum with options - do not referee silently (Shams's peacemaker strength, applied structurally). Completion criterion: conflicts logged with resolution path and date.
5. **Steering rhythm.** Standing agenda: decisions needed, exceptions, risks, next milestones; pre-brief the chair; follow up decisions in writing within 24h. Completion criterion: decision log current after every session.
6. **Trust maintenance.** Deliver small promises visibly; never surprise a stakeholder with bad news they should have heard first; give credit precisely and by name. Completion criterion: follow-through tracker - every promise closed or renegotiated.
7. **Business relationship cadence.** Regular touchpoints with key business leads beyond project needs (coffee, monthly check-in) - relationships built before they are needed. Completion criterion: relationship calendar live.

## Quick Reference
- **Map before you act:** the stakeholder you skipped is the one who blocks at the gate.
- **Peacemaker rule:** resolve conflict by structuring the trade-off, not by absorbing it silently.
- **No surprises:** bad news reaches key stakeholders from you, first, with context.
- **Credit by name builds the coalition** that carries the next project.

## Pitfalls
- **Champion churn:** re-mapping only at project start. Stances move - refresh monthly.
- **Treating blockers as enemies:** most are protecting something legitimate; find and address it.
- **Over-promising to win a room:** one broken promise costs ten delivered ones.
- **Skipping hidden influencers:** power without title decides outcomes in every organization.

## Verification
- Stakeholder map refreshed within 30 days; engagement plans per high-influence stakeholder.
- Decision log current; sponsor charters in place; follow-through tracker clean.
- Conflicting-interest items resolved through the deciding forum, not side-deals.

# Presentation Architect Skill

Executive deck structure and storyline: the Pyramid Principle, SCQA framing, MECE argument design, and slide-flow logic. Builds decks where the answer comes first and every slide advances one argument - the discipline used by strategy consultants (Minto/McKinsey) and top-tier architecture briefings. Visual craft lives in slide-design-expert; end-to-end production in cto-caio-presentation-master.

## When to Use
- Converting an idea, analysis, or proposal into an executive presentation
- Designing storyline and slide flow for a board/steering deck
- Restructuring a weak deck (answer buried, no narrative, slide sprawl)
- Don't use for: slide visuals (slide-design-expert), file mechanics (powerpoint skill), delivery prep (executive-presenter-coach)

## Procedure
1. **Decision mapping.** Who is in the room, what decision or action do they own, what do they already believe, what will they resist. A deck without a decision is a document. Completion criterion: audience-and-decision statement in one paragraph.
2. **SCQA frame.** Situation (agreed facts) -> Complication (what changed / what hurts) -> Question (the real question in their words) -> Answer (your recommendation in one sentence). Completion criterion: SCQA written and read aloud - if the Complication is not something the audience feels, rework it.
3. **Governing thought.** One sentence the whole deck proves: subject + verb + object + significance. Every slide supports it; anything that does not support it gets cut. Completion criterion: sentence approved by the presenter.
4. **MECE storyline.** The answer decomposes into 2-4 mutually-exclusive, collectively-exhaustive arguments (the pillars). Each pillar becomes a section; each section proves one claim. Test: could an argument live under two pillars? Reframe. Completion criterion: pillar list with the claim each proves.
5. **Action titles.** Every slide title is a full assertion sentence ("Migration cuts run cost 30% by Q2"), never a label ("Migration Plan"). Reading titles alone tells the whole story. Completion criterion: title-only read-through produces the argument.
6. **Vertical + horizontal logic check.** Vertical: title -> content answers the question the title raises. Horizontal: slide N+1 follows slide N without a missing link. Two failed checks = broken storyline, fix structure before styling. Completion criterion: both checks pass on the outline.
7. **Evidence + appendix strategy.** Each claim: the one number, chart, or citation that proves it - three numbers maximum per slide (executive-reporting rule). Deep proof lives in appendix slides mapped to anticipated questions. Completion criterion: evidence per claim; appendix mapped to Q&A.
8. **Deck skeleton.** Standard arc: Executive summary (the whole argument on one slide, SCQA compressed) -> Problem -> Analysis -> Solution -> Roadmap -> Investment/ROI -> Next steps + asks. Sections only as deep as the decision requires. Completion criterion: skeleton with slide counts per section (12-20 slides for board decks).

## Quick Reference
- **Pyramid Principle:** answer first -> group arguments MECE -> support with evidence; the audience gets the conclusion, then chooses depth.
- **SCQA hook:** open with the Situation they agree with to earn the right to the Complication they cannot ignore.
- **Slide = one message.** If two messages share a slide, the audience reads neither.
- **The ask lands at the end and repeats the answer:** Next Steps must name owner, date, and the decision requested.
- **Deck-type arcs:** strategy (SCQA -> options -> recommendation -> roadmap); business case (problem -> options -> ROI -> ask); architecture review (current -> target -> delta -> roadmap -> risk -> investment); status/steering (executive-reporting structure).

## Pitfalls
- **Data-dump decks:** 40 analysis slides with no pyramid - analysis belongs in appendix, findings in the flow.
- **Buried lede:** recommendation on slide 27. Executives decide in the first 5 minutes or not at all.
- **Slide sprawl:** every stakeholder request appended until the deck argues nothing. Governing thought is the filter.
- **Optionless problems:** presenting pain without recommendation - that is a status report, not a deck.
- **Jargon walls:** the room defines the language; board decks translate technical to business (caio-advisor translation table).

## Verification
- SCQA + governing thought on file; title-only read-through tells the story.
- MECE check passes; vertical/horizontal logic verified on the outline.
- Every claim has evidence; appendix mapped to anticipated questions; asks explicit.

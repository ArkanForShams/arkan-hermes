# Executive Presenter Coach Skill

Speaker preparation for executive presentations: per-slide speaker notes, presentation scripts with time budgets, board talking points, anticipated Q&A with layered answers, and rehearsal procedure. The delivery layer - pairs with executive-coach (general meeting presence) and presentation-architect (deck structure).

## When to Use
- Preparing to deliver a steering, CIO/CTO review, or board deck
- Generating speaker notes and a timed script from a finished deck
- Preparing Q&A: anticipated questions, layered answers, deflection patterns
- Rehearsal planning and feedback
- Don't use for: deck structure (presentation-architect), visuals (slide-design-expert), general meeting prep (executive-coach)

## Procedure
1. **Time budget first.** Slot length - 5 min buffer before Q&A; per-section minute allocation by importance (executive summary and ask get the most). Completion criterion: minute map per section; total = slot minus buffer.
2. **Speaker notes per slide.** Format: message (the one sentence), transition (bridge from previous), key point delivery, time budget, optional story/number to land. Notes live in the file's notes pane - not paper shuffled on stage. Completion criterion: every slide has a note with message + transition + time.
3. **Layered Q&A prep.** For the 8-10 most likely questions: headline answer (one sentence) -> supporting points (three max) -> appendix slide reference. Contested topics get explicit treatment: acknowledge -> evidence -> path forward. Completion criterion: Q&A document with layered answers; appendix mapped.
4. **Hard-question patterns.** Hostile or unknown questions: pause two seconds, "That is the right question" -> answer at the layer you own -> "what I can take back and confirm by [date]" for the unknown - never guess in front of a board. Completion criterion: patterns rehearsed; offline-commit list ready.
5. **Script + cut list.** Full script for the opening 90 seconds and closing ask (memorized verbatim); middle delivered from notes, not script. Cut list: which slides get skipped when the slot shrinks - agreed in advance, not improvised. Completion criterion: opening/closing memorized; cut list marked in the file.
6. **Rehearsal cycle.** Run 1: content check (read-through). Run 2: timed, standing, with slides - overruns cut via cut list. Run 3: with a listener (ARKAN can simulate the panel) - feedback on pace, filler words, eye contact. Completion criterion: timed run within budget; feedback pass completed.
7. **Delivery techniques.** Speak to the decision-makers not the screen; pause after key numbers; three-point answers (executive-coach structure); land the ask, then stop talking - silence after the ask is strength. Completion criterion: techniques checked in rehearsal run 3.
8. **Debrief loop.** Post-presentation: what landed, what dragged, which questions hit the prep and which missed - feed misses back into the Q&A prep pattern for next time. Completion criterion: debrief logged within 24 hours (executive-coach loop).

## Quick Reference
- **The ask, then silence.** Presenters who keep talking after the ask negotiate against themselves.
- **Notes are cues, not sentences:** reading slides or notes word-for-word kills presence.
- **Every technical question gets a layered answer:** headline -> three points -> appendix reference. Never open the appendix during the main flow.
- **Pace:** ~130-150 words per minute; nervousness accelerates - rehearsed pauses are the brake.
- **Boardroom math:** 20 slides in 20 minutes is too many; 10-12 with notes is a working deck.

## Pitfalls
- **Over-rehearsed openings collapsing:** memorize the opening and closing, internalize the middle - rigid middles break on the first interruption.
- **Appendix roulette:** searching for the slide you hope exists. Map every anticipated question to its appendix slide beforehand.
- **Reading the room wrong:** steering meetings want decisions; briefings want narrative - check which mode the meeting is in (executive-reporting).
- **Ignoring the time cut:** running long signals poor control. The cut list exists for exactly this moment.
- **Skipping rehearsal because "the deck is ready":** the deck being ready is why rehearsal matters - now the words are the only variable.

## Verification
- Notes on every slide; timed rehearsal within budget; opening/closing memorized.
- Q&A document covers the likely 8-10 questions with layered answers and appendix mapping.
- Debrief logged; misses fed into the next prep cycle.

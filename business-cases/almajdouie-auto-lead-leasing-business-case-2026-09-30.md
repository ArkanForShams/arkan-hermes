## 1. Executive Summary & Recommendation (read this first)

AlMajdouie Motors sells vehicles; it does not yet own the recurring mobility annuity that sits on top of vehicle ownership. The Saudi long-term leasing pool is the fastest-growing recurring-revenue pool in the automotive value chain — **SAR 4.0bn (FY24) → SAR 4.3bn (2025E), forecast to SAR 8.55bn by 2031 (~11.3% CAGR)** — and it is consolidating: four listed operators (Budget Saudi, Theeb, Lumi, Cherry) already hold ~53% of industry revenue and ~58% of the leased fleet. Every year without a leasing/fleet annuity line, AlMajdouie Motors captures a shrinking share of its own customers' lifetime spend, and its best fleet buyers (e.g., Yelo, with 15,500+ Changan and 5,000+ Hyundai/Genesis units supplied) build their margin on AlMajdouie's product instead of the reverse.

**Recommendation: Option A+ (Corporate-led operating lease first, consumer subscription second).** Stand up "Auto Lead Leasing" as a dedicated fleet-services entity: Phase 1 (2027) a corporate/government operating-lease fleet of ~1,000–1,500 vehicles anchored in group demand and 2–3 anchor corporate contracts; Phase 2 (2028–29) add the consumer-facing Quick Lead subscription and Fix Lead service-lease bundle once unit economics are proven on ~250 pilot cars. Full consumer + financing stack (Option C) is deliberately deferred to a gated Phase 3 (post-2029) requiring board approval at a later date.

Indicative Phase 1–2 investment: **~SAR 60–65M equity + fleet financing line** (details §5). Exit-year (Y2) run-rate revenue: **~SAR 40–56 M**, growing to a realistic ~2–3% of the 2031 leasing pool (SAR 170–255M) by 2030–31 if Phase 2 executes. The investment is material but bounded, uses assets the group already owns (brands, workshops, used-car outlet, corporate relationships), and avoids the two highest-risk moves (a SAMA finance-license build, and a consumer brand war against Budget/Lumi/Theeb) in year one.

**Decision requested at the end of this document:** approve Phase 1 funding and mandate a feasibility gate in Q1 2027.

## 2. Market Reality (what the numbers say)

### 2.1 Size and growth
- Car rental + leasing market: **USD 2.3–2.9bn in 2025** depending on definition (Mordor/MarqStats/Ken Research 2025–26 editions); consensus forecast CAGR **7–9%** to 2030–32. Long-term (operational) leasing is the **fastest-growing segment in every report**.
- Long-term leasing pool specifically: grew from **SAR 2.1bn (FY20) to SAR 3.7bn (FY24)**; fleet-leasing revenue ~**SAR 4.3bn (2025E) → SAR 8.55bn (2031F)** with fleet growing ~89.9% to ~356,900 vehicles by 2031 (AlJazira Capital Feb 2026; MarqStats 2026).
- Short-term rental: **SAR 4.0bn FY24**, expected 5.7% CAGR FY25–29 — tourism-driven but more cyclical and more competitively saturated (750+ operators).
- Demand drivers named consistently across sources: Vision 2030 giga-project contractor fleets, corporate & government fleet outsourcing (institutional customers = ~76.4% of forecast fleet growth to 2031), SME adoption (+22.6% of net fleet growth coming from SMEs), tourism (123M tourist trips in 2025), digital channel shift (36% → 72% by 2032).

### 2.2 Competitive structure (the honest picture)
- **Budget Saudi (~23% of lease fleet, 43,700 vehicles), Theeb (14.1%), Lumi (12.5%), Cherry (8.3%)** — together ≈58% of fleet, 66% of lease revenue (2025E). Top-4 listed players hold ~53% of total industry revenue; the sector is consolidating ahead of listings.
- Competitive friction is currently intense: pre-IPO volume race has created **temporary pricing pressure in both rental and leasing** (AlJazira, Feb 2026). Budget grew revenue 27.5% (9M 2025); Theeb 14.9% (2025); Theeb took 6.5% long-term leasing share within five years — proof that share is winnable, but mostly by scaled, listed players with cheap capital.
- Unit economics benchmarks (2024–25): revenue/vehicle **SAR 22,900 national average** (SAR 33,445 commercial vs 20,982 passenger); depreciation/vehicle SAR 10.4–12.8k; **purchase-price recovery on remarketing 56–70%** (Lumi lowest, Theeb highest); utilization improving 74% → 79% by 2032. Note: these are the benchmarks Auto Lead must meet or beat.

### 2.3 The gap AlMajdouie sits in
- AlMajdouie Motors: exclusive distributor Hyundai (E+N regions since 1985, largest Hyundai showroom worldwide per company material), Genesis, Peugeot, Citroën, Changan (>150,000 Changan cars on Saudi roads), Deepal, Genesis, plus Chery Commercial (RELY/Karry launched May 2026); Auto Lead-branded oil-change centers; "Almajdouie Motors" automotive-finance leadership role exists in-house.
- **The strategic gap:** the group supplies fleets to lessors (Yelo: >15,500 Changan + >5,000 Hyundai/Genesis) but does not keep the lease annuity, the telematics/fleet-management data, or the remarketing margin. A distributor with a leasing arm captures four profit layers instead of one: vehicle margin → lease yield → service/maintenance → used-car remarketing.
- Counter-position risk to manage: Auto Lead Leasing would compete for corporate deals against its own fleet customer Yelo (and other rental firms) — product segmentation (corporate full-service lease vs short-term rental) and internal firewalls must be designed in from day one.

### 2.4 Adjacent pools
- Used-car market: **USD 24.5bn (2025), 1.6M transactions, growing 7.4% CAGR**; organized-channel share rising — this is where lease return assets get monetized and where Auto Lead's "Used Lead" exits end.
- New-vehicle sales ~854,770 units (2025); operating parc ~15.8M (2024) — replacement pool is enormous and growing.
- EV transition: Riyadh 30% EV target by 2030, Ceer (Q4 2026 production), Hyundai-PIF plant (2026) — a leasing fleet is one of the **lowest-risk EV adoption channels** (fleet trial before consumer purchase); AlMajdouie's alfanar EV-charging partnership (May 2026) is directly relevant collateral.

### 2.5 Regulation (verify before Phase 1 contract signing — do not rely on this summary)
- Rental/leasing activity is **TGA-licensed**: unified electronic contract, activity licensing, and minimum fleet thresholds (Ken Research cites "Category B ≥ 300 vehicles under current regulation"). Confirm exact current categories with TGA.
- Pure **operating lease to corporates does not require a SAMA finance license**; but if "Fix Lead" evolves into installment sales/finance lease to consumers, that activity is **SAMA-licensed** (finance lease / consumer finance are named license activities; ~64 licensed finance companies as of Jan 2025, including dealer-group precedents Taajeer and AlJabr). **Regulatory verdicts belong to Compliance/Legal — get a written SAMA scoping opinion before any consumer financing product.**

## 3. Product-to-Market Mapping (Auto Lead's four lines)

| Auto Lead line | Product | Market segment | Regulatory path | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Auto Lease** | Corporate operating lease, full service | Corporates, government, giga-project contractors (87% of leased fleet demand) | TGA license | **Phase 1 — core** |
| **Quick Lead** | Monthly consumer subscription (insurance+maintenance+registration bundled) | Individuals, SMEs, new arrivals, gig workers | TGA; review SAMA scope with legal | Phase 2 |
| **Fix Lead** | Service/repair subscription; deferred-service plans on leased fleet | Existing AMC customers (Hyundai/Changan base) | Consumer service contract — legal review; if credit-embedded → SAMA | Phase 2 (bundle add-on) |
| **Used Lead** | Certified pre-owned outlet for lease returns | End-of-term remarketing, C2B/C2C | Dealer rules (MC) | Phase 1 support function |

Logic: Auto Lease builds the fleet asset base; Used Lead guarantees the exit channel; Quick/Fix monetize the digital front-end on top of a fleet that already exists.

## 4. Strategic Fit — Why this, why AlMajdouie, why now
1. **Annuity stacking:** converts episodic vehicle sales into 1–5-year contracted revenue (long-term contracts already ~45% of listed lessors' revenue mix, per GIB Capital via Ken Research).
2. **Demand the group controls:** AlMajdouie group companies (logistics, food distribution, industrial, real estate) are anchor operating-lease customers — an internal demand base competitors must win through tenders.
3. **Full-margin loop:** lease → in-network maintenance (Auto Lead oil-change centers, Hyundai/Changan service capacity: >100k vehicles/yr serviced) → remarketing via Used Lead at benchmark 56–70% recovery.
4. **Vision 2030 alignment:** mobility-as-a-service, EV fleet transition (Deepal/alfanar assets), Saudization of service talent.
5. **Defensive timing:** top-4 consolidation is accelerating; the middle market (SMEs, second-tier corporates, regional cities) is where a new operator can win without fighting Budget/Lumi/Theeb head-on.

## 5. The Three Options

*(All financials are analyst estimates built on the cited benchmarks; fleet capex = equity share ~35% of ~SAR 90k average vehicle cost, remainder fleet financing; revenue at ~SAR 27k/vehicle blended.)*

### Option A — Corporate-led operating lease (Auto Lease + Used Lead)
- **Scope:** TGA-licensed fleet entity; 1,000–1,500 vehicles by end-Y2 (~0.7% of the 2028 leasing pool); 2–3 anchor corporate contracts + group internal demand; maintenance in-network; remarketing via Used Lead.
- **Investment:** ~**SAR 47M** fleet equity + **SAR 15M** ops/technology (telematics, lease management system, sales team) → **~SAR 60–65M** plus a fleet financing line/facility (floorplan).
- **Y2 run-rate revenue:** **~SAR 40.5M** (≈ SAR 23–33k/vehicle). Path to ~2% share of 2031 pool = SAR 171M.
- **Time to first revenue:** 9–12 months (contract-dependent).
- **Pros:** lowest capital, uses existing assets, no consumer war, no SAMA exposure, internal anchor demand, fastest breakeven.
- **Cons:** does not build consumer brand; growth bounded by B2B sales capacity; corporate tender cycles are slow.

### Option B — Consumer-first subscription (Quick Lead + Fix Lead)
- **Scope:** digital-first car subscription app; ~1,000 subscriber vehicles by Y2 at ~SAR 1,300/month blended *(assumption)* incl. insurance & maintenance; Fix Lead service plans upsold.
- **Investment:** **SAR 31.5M** fleet equity + **SAR 20M** digital platform (license or build) → **~SAR 50M**.
- **Y2 run-rate revenue:** **~SAR 15.6M** (+ service attach). Subscription economics need proven churn (<4%/mo) and utilization (>75%) — both unproven for a new brand.
- **Pros:** highest NPS/brand upside; sticky recurring consumer relationships; matches digital-channel shift (72% by 2032).
- **Cons:** brand-building vs giants with airport counters; churn risk; residual-value risk concentrated in consumer-driven vehicle mix; platform opex before volume. **As a first move, this is the weakest option — do not lead with it.**

### Option C — Full-stack mobility (all four lines + financing)
- **Scope:** everything in A+B plus Quick Lead scale (3,000 vehicles), Fix Lead as financing product, captive finance-lease desk.
- **Investment:** ~**SAR 94.5M** fleet equity + **SAR 90M** ops/tech/brand → **~SAR 180–200M**; Y2 run-rate **~SAR 81M** (≈1.4% of 2031-adjacent pool).
- **Regulatory:** captive consumer financing requires **SAMA finance-company licensing** (Finance Companies Control Law track; 18–36 months typical; capital requirements apply). This is the single largest regulator-dependent step.
- **Pros:** full annuity capture; optionality to IPO like Budget/Lumi/Theeb; group-wide synergies.
- **Cons:** double the capital and execution load; regulatory timeline risk; brand war on two fronts; needs a leadership hire (fleet-services MD) that does not exist in-house today.

### Comparative summary (Y2 exit view)
| Metric | A (Corporate-first) | B (Consumer-first) | C (Full-stack) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital required | ~SAR 60–65M | ~SAR 50M | ~SAR 180–200M |
| Y2 run-rate revenue | ~SAR 40.5M | ~SAR 15.6M | ~SAR 81M |
| Regulatory risk | Low (TGA) | Medium | High (SAMA) |
| Asset reuse | High (workshops, dealer network, brands) | Medium | Highest |
| Execution complexity | Medium | High (digital ops) | Very high |
| Fit with current capabilities | **Strongest** | Weakest | 3–5 yr ambition |

## 6. Risks (with mitigations)
1. **Pre-IPO price war / margin pressure** — Budget/Lumi/Theeb are buying share. → Segment discipline: target SMEs/second-tier corporates and regional markets they under-serve; price on service bundles, not day-rates.
2. **Residual-value risk** — remarketing exposure at contract end; Chinese-brand price pressure (12% of new sales already Chinese) could deflate used values. → Shorter cycles for volatile segments, remarketing via Used Lead/OTB channels, residual risk-sharing clauses with OEMs (AlMajdouie sits on the buy side — a structural advantage peers lack).
3. **Channel conflict with Yelo** and other fleet customers. → Formal product segmentation + internal governance note; leasing desk targets corporate full-service, not short-term rental.
4. **Regulatory creep** — "Fix Lead" deferred-payment structures may reclassify as consumer finance. → Written SAMA scoping opinion before launch of any payment-plan product; verdicts owned by Compliance/Legal.
5. **Capital intensity + financing cost** — fleet funding line cost is rate-sensitive. → Secure an Islamic facilities line (ijara-based) with the group bank before launch; hedge via contracts.
6. **EV transition residuals** (30% Riyadh EV target 2030) — lease book could hold stranded ICE residuals or immature EV residuals. → Cap EV share of Phase 1 fleet at pilot levels; leverage Deepal + alfanar charging as controlled experiments.
7. **Execution capacity** — the group is simultaneously integrating Chery commercial brands, Hyundai GDSI 2.0 facilities. → Dedicated P&L owner (MD hire), board-gated phase approvals, no Phase 2 launch before Phase 1 KPIs (§8).
8. **Demand-side substitution** — ride-hailing + Riyadh Metro pressure on short-term rental economics (ANB Capital flags this). → Weight toward multi-year corporate contracts, which are structurally insulated.

## 8. Timeline & "Business Excellence" G *(phase-gated)*
- **Phase 0 — Governance & Feasibility (Q4 2026):** executive review; TGA license pre-check; SAMA scoping memo (compliance); anchor corporate LOIs; business case v2 with real group demand data. *Gate: LOIs ≥ 500 vehicles.*
- **Phase 1 — Build & Corporate Pilot (H1 2027):** entity + license; lease management system + telematics; 250-vehicle pilot with 2–3 anchor contracts; Used Lead return channel live. *Gate: utilization ≥ 75%, negative unit-level EBITDA gap < SAR 4k/vehicle/yr.*
- **Phase 2 — Scale Corporate + Launch Consumer (H2 2027–2028):** fleet to 1,500; Quick Lead subscription pilot (200–500 vehicles); Fix Lead bundled service plans on all Auto Lease contracts. *Gate: churn < 4%/mo; remarketing recovery ≥ 60%.*
- **Phase 3 — Financing Layer & Option C decision (2029+):** evaluate SAMA license vs partner/bank partnership; Quick Lead scale; regional expansion. *Gate: Phase 2 P&L proven.*
- **Excellence KPI scoreboard (continuous):** fleet utilization ≥ 80%; revenue/vehicle ≥ SAR 23k; maintenance cost/vehicle vs benchmark; days-to-remarket ≤ 45; purchase-price recovery ≥ 60–65%; net promoter score ≥ 60 (corporate); system/telematics data coverage = 100%. *(Benchmarks from §2.2 data.)*

## 9. Pros & Cons of the Recommended Path (A-first, gated to C)
**Pros:** bounded capital; uses brands, workshops, OEM pricing power, and internal demand nobody else has; avoids SAMA timeline in Phase 1; builds remarketing + telematics data that Phase 3 financing will need; consistent with halal finance structuring (ijara-based fleet funding; no consumer-credit products before Shariah + SAMA clearance); preserves optionality to stop at Phase 2.
**Cons:** a consumer brand (Quick Lead) is delayed while digital-native competitors court individual customers; if top-4 keep consolidating, the middle-market window narrows; requires MD hire and PMO capacity in an organization already executing multi-brand projects; two-phase gating adds governance friction (~2 quarters) before consumer scale-up.

---

## 10. Shariah Structuring Note *(research input — not a verdict)*
Ijara-style operating leases and service subscriptions are structurally permissible business models; the compliance risk sits in funding (interest-based fleet finance → use Islamic facilities), insurance (takaful vs conventional), late-fee treatment, and any deferred-payment consumer product. Final structuring must go to the group's Shariah board/compliance and, for licensed activity, SAMA. **No consumer financing product launches before both signs off.**

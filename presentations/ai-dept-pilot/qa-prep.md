# Q&A Preparation — AI Department 90-Day Pilot Briefing

**Format:** headline answer (one sentence) → supporting points (max 3) → appendix/reference.

## Q1. "How is this different from the AI tools people already use?"
**Headline:** We are not adding tools — we are organizing the ones already in use under governance and measurement.
- Today: ad-hoc usage, no owner, no measured value, ungoverned data handling.
- The pilot adds: scored use-case portfolio, tier gates, weekly value ledger.
- Nothing already working gets taken away — it gets adopted into a lane.

## Q2. "What does this cost?"
**Headline:** Incremental cash cost is small; the real investment is reallocated staff time.
- Platform already owned (SAR 0 incremental); tooling SAR 2–4k/month for pilot scope (sourcing to confirm).
- ~2 FTE-equivalent, reallocated from existing reporting/support workload — not new headcount.
- All figures are estimates with assumptions stated — the pilot's first act is measuring the real baseline.

## Q3. "How do we know the ROI is real?"
**Headline:** We don't promise it — we measure it, which is exactly why the pilot is 90 days with a kill criterion.
- Baseline measured in weeks 1–2 BEFORE anything scales (status reporting, meeting summaries, ticket triage).
- Weekly value ledger; day-90 review vs baseline; 30–50% time reduction is the target range, stated as an assumption.
- No measured value by day 90 → we stop, with lessons documented. The downside is capped by design.

## Q4. "What about data security and compliance?"
**Headline:** Every system passes a risk-tier gate aligned with group IT policy before it touches anything sensitive.
- Three tiers with proportionate controls; Tier 3 requires security review + data assessment + human approval gate.
- Data-flow map per system before deployment; no personal or payment data without explicit approval.
- Audit trail: any system's tier, owner, and gate status retrievable in 5 minutes.

## Q5. "Will this replace our people?"
**Headline:** No — it removes the reporting and coordination load so the team works on the systems themselves.
- Assist-first adoption: AI drafts, humans review and decide (autonomy ladder with human gates on irreversible actions).
- Staff time is reallocated, not reduced; the pilot's success metric includes team adoption, not just hours saved.
- Engineers keep core domain work by rotation — AI accelerates boilerplate, never replaces judgment.

## Q6. "Why 90 days — why not start bigger?"
**Headline:** Because a bounded pilot with kill criteria is how you earn the right to scale.
- Three phases, each ending at a decision gate; leadership retains control at every gate.
- Scaling before measurement is how AI programs burn trust (pilot purgatory is the industry's default failure).
- If the value is proven at day 90, the scale-up case writes itself — with our own numbers.

## Q7. "Who is accountable if something goes wrong?"
**Headline:** I am — the department leads it, with named owners for every risk and gate.
- Risk table (slide 10): data exposure, value tracking, team disruption, shadow AI — each has a named owner and mitigation.
- Incident runbook for Tier 2+ systems: pause, notify, preserve evidence, post-incident review.
- Reporting goes through the existing PMO/steering cadence — no parallel shadow reporting.

## Q8. "What do you need from leadership today?"
**Headline:** One decision: approve the 90-day pilot with its three governance gates.
- GO / ADJUST / NO-GO — adjust means scope or gates change, not the deadline.
- Day-90 review returns to this room with measured results.
- If NO-GO: the alternative is continued ungoverned adoption — the exposure grows either way; the pilot makes it managed.
